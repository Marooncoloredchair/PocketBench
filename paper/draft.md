## Abstract

Generative models for structure-based drug design (SBDD) are evaluated almost exclusively on molecular output quality — validity, uniqueness, and drug-likeness computed from a single fixed featurization of the target binding pocket. This design measures what models can generate but not whether their outputs are reliable under the input variation that drug discovery workflows routinely introduce. Here we introduce a perturbation-based reliability benchmark and apply it to DiffSBDD across two independent panels of diverse protein–ligand complexes from the RCSB Protein Data Bank (n = 47 and n = 99). We separate two classes of perturbation that are usually conflated: *featurization noise* that should leave the binding hypothesis unchanged (atom-order shuffle, sub-ångström coordinate jitter), and *pocket boundary redefinition* that changes which residues are presented to the model (±1.5 Å crop-radius adjustment). On normalized chemistry metrics (validity, uniqueness, mean and standard deviation of QED), DiffSBDD is **robust** to featurization noise: brittleness is **≤3%** at a conservative dispersion threshold (τ = 0.10) on both panels. The same model is **systematically sensitive** to pocket boundary definition. Contracting the crop radius by 1.5 Å degrades quantitative drug-likeness (QED) in **54%** of pockets, with a significant and large paired effect on both panels (Wilcoxon signed-rank, p = 5.3 × 10⁻⁶ at n = 47 and p = 2.3 × 10⁻¹⁰ at n = 99; matched-pairs rank-biserial r ≈ −0.72 and −0.73), whereas synthetic accessibility shows no significant shift. We distil this into a reusable **Pocket Boundary Sensitivity Index** (the QED–vs–crop-radius slope, reported relative to each model's featurization-noise floor): for **74–78% of pockets the boundary swing exceeds the noise floor**, so the cropping policy moves drug-likeness more than representational noise does. The headline that an earlier, unnormalized brittleness flag assigned every pocket a brittleness rate of 1.0 is an artefact of mixing raw molecule counts and unnormalized SA scores into a threshold tuned for [0,1] metrics; once metrics are placed on a comparable scale, the apparent universal fragility disappears and a specific, reproducible sensitivity to pocket geometry remains. The central message is that **pocket boundary definition matters more than featurization noise, and current SBDD benchmarks test neither**. We argue that leaderboards should report generation coverage, brittleness on normalized metrics, and explicit sensitivity to pocket-definition policy alongside conventional quality scores.

## Introduction

Generative structure-based drug design seeks ligands that complement a resolved or predicted binding site [1]. Contemporary pipelines couple geometric deep learning with molecular sampling and are benchmarked almost exclusively on static pocket coordinates: researchers report distributions of validity, synthesizability, drug-likeness, and downstream proxy tasks on held-out targets [2]. Those evaluations answer what molecules a model proposes on a given representation; they do not, without further tests, answer whether those proposals are *reliable* when the ostensibly same pocket is re-encoded through orderings, noise, or cropping policies that leave chemical identity nominally unchanged [3].

The benchmark we present is open source and reproducible: code, configuration, archived metrics, and figure-generation scripts are packaged together so independent groups can rerun the same perturbation protocol and comparative analyses.

Reliability matters for drug discovery because structural biology workflows routinely expose models to heterogeneous preprocessing choices: alternate atom orderings in PDB-derived tensors, coordinate noise within experimental uncertainty, and geometric cropping heuristics that define “the pocket.” If benchmark scores swing under such featurization-preserving variants, reported performance conflates molecular competence with sensitivity to arbitrary pocket definition. The methodological gap is that mainstream SBDD benchmark suites emphasize cross-target generalization and scalar quality metrics on **static** pocket encodings, not stress tests that deliberately re-encode the ostensibly same site through ordering noise, coordinate jitter, or pocket-definition heuristics.

We address this gap with a controlled protocol that **separates two perturbation classes that prior reliability discussions conflate**. *Featurization noise* — atom-order shuffle and sub-ångström coordinate jitter — varies how a fixed binding site is encoded without changing which residues define it; a reliable model should be nearly invariant. *Pocket boundary redefinition* — ±1.5 Å crop-radius adjustment — changes which heavy atoms enter the pocket tensor and therefore encodes a genuinely different, but equally defensible, definition of "the pocket"; sensitivity here exposes dependence on an arbitrary preprocessing policy rather than on chemistry. We regenerate molecules per condition and quantify (i) brittleness on **normalized** chemistry metrics under featurization noise and (ii) the **directional, paired effect** of pocket contraction on drug-likeness and synthesizability. We instantiate the protocol on two independent panels (n = 47 and n = 99) with DiffSBDD, report normalized brittleness versus threshold (Fig. 5, Table 1), quantify the crop-radius effect with paired Wilcoxon signed-rank tests (Table 2), and retain a twenty-pocket **meaningful mutation** benchmark with optional Vina rescoring (Fig. 6). We also report the earlier all-column brittleness flag as a cautionary secondary result (Figs. 1–4): it is dominated by raw molecule counts and unnormalized SA scores and should not be read as universal fragility.

## Results

### DiffSBDD is robust to featurization noise

We evaluated DiffSBDD on two independent panels — **n = 47** and **n = 99** diverse RCSB complexes — using one reference pocket and four perturbation conditions per target (Methods). Two conditions are *featurization noise* (atom-order shuffle, sub-ångström coordinate jitter) that re-encode a fixed binding site; two adjust crop radius and therefore **change which heavy atoms enter the pocket tensor** (treated separately as pocket-boundary perturbations).

Brittleness is the fraction of pockets for which the standard deviation of any monitored metric across the four perturbation conditions exceeds a dispersion threshold τ. Restricting the monitored set to **normalized chemistry metrics on a comparable [0,1] scale** — validity, uniqueness, and the mean and standard deviation of QED — DiffSBDD is **robust**: at the conservative default **τ = 0.10**, brittleness is **0.0% on the 47-pocket panel (0/47) and 3.0% on the 99-pocket panel (3/99)**, i.e. **≤3%** on both (`paper/normalized_brittleness.csv`; Table 1). Brittleness rises only at the stricter τ = 0.05 (21% and 35%) and vanishes (0%) at τ ≥ 0.15. The featurization noise that prior framing treated as a universal failure mode leaves normalized chemistry essentially unchanged once metrics are placed on the same scale.

### Pocket boundary contraction systematically degrades drug-likeness

The picture inverts for **pocket boundary redefinition**. Contracting the crop radius by 1.5 Å (`crop_radius_minus_1.5`) — a change of preprocessing policy that re-defines which residues constitute "the pocket" — lowers per-pocket quantitative drug-likeness in roughly half of all targets: QED drops by more than 0.05 in **51% of pockets (n = 47)** and **54% of pockets (n = 99)** (`paper/crop_radius_effect.csv`). The shift is directional and reproducible across both panels. A paired Wilcoxon signed-rank test on per-pocket ΔQED (`crop_radius_minus_1.5` − `original`) is **highly significant with a large effect on both panels**: **W = 159, p = 5.3 × 10⁻⁶, rank-biserial r = −0.72** (n = 47) and **W = 658, p = 2.3 × 10⁻¹⁰, r = −0.73** (n = 99) (Table 2; `paper/crop_radius_wilcoxon.csv`). Bootstrap 95% confidence intervals on the median ΔQED exclude zero on both panels (n = 47: [−0.072, −0.023]; n = 99: [−0.078, −0.034]). The most affected targets lose 0.12–0.26 QED outright (for example **7G6A** 0.655 → 0.398 and **9CJT** 0.499 → 0.257 on the 99-pocket panel; **2AV1** 0.524 → 0.349 on the 47-pocket panel). That a 1.5 Å change in an analyst's cropping heuristic — well within the range of defensible pocket definitions — moves drug-likeness this much, this consistently, is the central reliability concern.

### Synthetic accessibility does not track pocket boundary changes

The same paired test on ΔSA (synthetic accessibility) shows **no significant effect** of pocket contraction on either panel (n = 47: p = 0.96, r = +0.01; n = 99: p = 0.22, r = +0.14; Table 2), and the median ΔSA confidence intervals straddle zero. The sensitivity to pocket boundary is therefore **specific to the drug-likeness objective**, not a uniform degradation of every chemistry metric — consistent with the model re-optimizing toward different (often smaller, lower-QED) chemotypes when the conditioning context shrinks, rather than producing globally worse molecules.

### A dose-response measure: the Pocket Boundary Sensitivity Index (PBSI)

To make boundary sensitivity a **quantity** that future work can report and compare — rather than a single ±1.5 Å contrast — we define the **Pocket Boundary Sensitivity Index (PBSI)**. For each pocket we regress mean QED on the crop-radius offset Δr (Å) and take the slope **b = dQED/dΔr**; the panel-level PBSI is the median |b| (Methods). Crucially, PBSI is reported **relative to the model's own featurization-noise floor**: for each pocket we compare the QED swing induced by boundary change against the standard deviation of QED across the atom-shuffle and coordinate-jitter conditions on that same pocket. This makes the central claim a *measurement* rather than an assertion.

Using the three crop radii already present in the benchmark (baseline 8 Å and ±1.5 Å) as a coarse dose-response, **PBSI = 0.018 QED/Å (n = 47) and 0.023 QED/Å (n = 99)**. The slope is positive — i.e. **shrinking the pocket lowers QED** — for **68% (n = 47) and 82% (n = 99)** of pockets, agreeing in direction with the paired Wilcoxon result. Most importantly, the boundary swing over a 1.5 Å crop change **exceeds the featurization-noise floor in 74% (n = 47) and 78% (n = 99) of pockets**, with a median signal-to-noise ratio of **1.7× and 2.6×** (`paper/pocket_boundary_sensitivity.csv`; Table 3). In other words, for roughly three-quarters of pockets, *which crop radius an analyst chooses changes drug-likeness more than the representational noise the field worries about*. The most sensitive pockets reach |b| ≈ 0.07–0.09 QED/Å (for example 9CJT, 9MER, 8PAV on the 99-pocket panel), losing up to a quarter of a QED unit across the tested radius range. A finer continuous sweep (radii 5–11 Å in 0.5 Å steps; `configs/diffsbdd_real100_cropsweep.yaml`, `analysis/make_crop_sweep_config.py`) is shipped to chart the full dose-response and locate per-pocket critical radii in follow-up runs.

### The "brittleness rate 1.0" headline is a metric-scaling artefact

An earlier analysis flagged **every** pocket brittle (brittleness rate 1.0 at τ ≤ 0.10) on both panels. We report this as a **secondary, cautionary observation**: the all-column flag mixed **raw molecule counts** (n_total, n_valid, unique valid-molecule count) and **unnormalized synthetic-accessibility scores** (mean SA on a ~1–10 scale, std SA) into a threshold tuned for [0,1] proportions. Under that flag, the dominant driver was the unique valid-molecule **count** for twenty-six pockets and **mean SA** for seventeen (Fig. 1) — quantities whose natural dispersion exceeds 0.10 regardless of model stability. Once the monitored set is normalized (above), the apparent universal fragility collapses to ≤3%. We retain the all-column traces (Figs. 1–4), including the validity excursion for PDB entry **3RFM** (0.85 → 0.50 under coordinate jitter) and pooled SA drift (Fig. 4), as examples of *isolated* large moves, but they do not support a claim of pervasive featurization brittleness.

### Threshold sensitivity (normalized metrics)

Brittleness classifications depend on τ. On normalized chemistry metrics, the rate falls monotonically with τ on both panels: from **21% / 35%** at τ = 0.05, to **0% / 3.0%** at τ = 0.10, to **0% / 0%** at τ ≥ 0.15 (47-pocket / 99-pocket; Fig. 5; Table 1). We adopt **τ = 0.10** as the conservative operational default: it represents a ten-point swing on unitless proportions and matches the |Δ| > 0.10 responsiveness cutoff used for meaningful mutations. At this default the featurization-noise brittleness is negligible on both panels, sharpening the contrast with the pocket-boundary effect.

### Meaningful side-chain perturbations (DiffSBDD, twenty-pocket panel)

We stress-tested DiffSBDD on **20 pockets** with **nearest-residue** **alanine** vs **tryptophan** substitutions that preserve the backbone but change local chemistry; the mutated residue is chosen by proximity to the reference ligand centroid (Methods). Tags `meaningful_ala` and `meaningful_trp` are **fixed across pockets** so summaries remain comparable despite different absolute residue IDs.

Pooling **40 pocket–mutation pairs** (20 × 2), we classified a pair as **responsive** if **|Δ| > 0.10** versus the **original** pocket in **at least one** of: validity, mean QED, mean SA, or mean **AutoDock Vina** score (20 Å box, pocket heavy-atom receptor; Methods). **Thirty-two pairs (80%)** were responsive; **eight** were not. Among responsive pairs, **65.6%** (21/32) passed a **QED–validity directionality** screen (no strong opposing shifts; see `analysis/meaningful_perturbation_analysis.py`), and **59.4%** (19/32) passed a coarse **Vina–QED** concordance check. **Alanine** mutations **predominantly shift mean Vina toward less negative kcal/mol** (predicted affinity **weakens**; positive Δ when defined as mutant − original), consistent with **trimming sidechain interaction surface**. **Tryptophan** responses were **variable** across pockets. **Three of twenty pockets** showed **no** responsive pair for **either** mutation—suggesting binding contexts **dominated by backbone or contact patterns** **insensitive** to identity at the nearest mutated sidechain.

Summary tables and a delta heatmap are in `paper/meaningful_perturbation_summary.csv` and Fig. 6 / `paper/figures/meaningful_deltas_heatmap.pdf`, updated after docking rescoring on archived SDFs (`run_diffsbdd_meaningful_real20`).

### Docking score sensitivity under meaningful perturbations

Vina **mean** scores (kcal/mol; **more negative** = more favorable) illustrate **pocket-specific** sensitivity. Representative **shifts** (mutant − original) include: **1B0R** original **−3.824** → alanine **−3.496** (**Δ = +0.328**); **5U98** **−4.045** → alanine **−3.541** (**Δ = +0.504**); **3UPR** **−3.913** → tryptophan **−4.255** (**Δ = −0.342**, **stronger** predicted binding in this case). These examples sit alongside the aggregate pattern (Ala tending toward **less negative** means; Trp heterogeneous) and underscore that **meaningful perturbations induce interpretable order-of-magnitude score motion** on par with property-metric shifts.

**Summary of reliability findings.** Taken together, the two perturbation classes dissociate cleanly. **DiffSBDD is robust to featurization noise** — atom-order shuffle and sub-ångström jitter that discovery workflows should collapse to a single binding hypothesis leave normalized chemistry metrics stable (≤3% brittleness at τ = 0.10 on both panels). The same model is **systematically sensitive to pocket boundary definition**: a 1.5 Å crop change degrades drug-likeness in roughly half of all pockets with a large, significant, replicated paired effect. The reliability problem in current SBDD is therefore **not** representational noise but the **arbitrary preprocessing policy that defines the pocket**, and the meaningful-mutation panel below shows that biological signal is, separately, only unevenly tracked.

**Pocket2Mol coverage caveat (aligned with `monday_honest_summary.md`).** An original **13/47** reference-pocket coverage figure from the first adapter pass was largely an **input-format artefact**: the bridge fed pocket-only cropped PDBs with pocket-centroid anchoring, whereas Pocket2Mol expects a **full protein PDB** plus a **ligand-centered** cubic bbox. A corrected re-benchmark is **in progress** (partial v2: 19/47 pockets run; 16/19 with valid originals — see `paper/pocket2mol_real47_coverage_v2.txt`). **No corrected full-panel coverage number is claimed.** Do not use 13/47 as a comparative or model-limited headline.

### The effect replicates across independent panels and sample counts

The crop-radius result is established on **two non-overlapping panels** (n = 47 and n = 99), each yielding a large, significant ΔQED effect in the same direction (Table 2), so it is not an artefact of a particular target set. Sampling budget is similarly not the driver: on the ten pockets with highest reference-pocket validity, repeating the conditions with **n = 50** rather than the panel default of **n = 20** left the normalized brittleness conclusions unchanged at τ ∈ {0.05, 0.10} (`paper/nsamples_sensitivity.csv`; annotated on Fig. 5).

## Discussion

The two perturbation classes give a clear and, we argue, more useful reading of SBDD reliability than a single brittleness headline. **Featurization noise that should be inconsequential is, in fact, largely inconsequential** for DiffSBDD once metrics are normalized: ≤3% of pockets show dispersion above τ = 0.10 on either panel. **Pocket boundary definition, which is usually treated as an innocuous preprocessing detail, is the dominant source of instability**: a 1.5 Å crop change degrades drug-likeness in ~54% of pockets with a large, significant, panel-replicated paired effect. The practical implication is uncomfortable. Two competent analysts who crop the same crystal structure with cropping radii 1.5 Å apart will, half the time, obtain meaningfully different drug-likeness for the generated molecules — not because the chemistry changed but because the pocket-definition policy did. **Current SBDD benchmarks test neither axis**: they score a single fixed featurization, so they neither certify invariance to benign noise nor expose sensitivity to pocket-definition choices.

These findings carry implications for **benchmark design**. Leaderboards should require (i) explicit generation coverage on the reference pocket, (ii) brittleness reported on **normalized** chemistry metrics (not raw counts or unnormalized SA, which inflate any [0,1]-tuned threshold), (iii) **sensitivity to pocket-definition policy** as a first-class axis — we propose the **Pocket Boundary Sensitivity Index** (PBSI), the QED–vs–crop-radius slope reported relative to a model's featurization-noise floor, as a compact, comparable summary — and (iv) tabulated threshold sensitivity rather than single-τ headlines. The corollary for practitioners is that the pocket-extraction radius should be reported as a hyperparameter and, ideally, marginalized over rather than fixed silently.

### Mechanistic hypotheses

SE(3)-equivariant diffusion models such as DiffSBDD enforce consistent predictions under global Euclidean rigid motions of the input point cloud, which explains the observed **robustness to coordinate jitter and atom-order shuffle**: small displacements and permutations either fall within the model's equivariance/invariance guarantees or perturb edge masks only marginally, leaving normalized chemistry stable. Changing the crop radius is categorically different — it **changes the input atom set** and therefore the conditioning distribution itself, so successive conditions are not related by any symmetry the architecture respects. A smaller pocket presents fewer anchoring contacts, and the model re-optimizes toward chemotypes that fit the reduced context; the directional QED loss (and the *absence* of a matched SA effect) is consistent with the model trading drug-likeness for geometric fit to a sparser pocket rather than degrading uniformly.

A complementary mechanism, raised by **Prof. Jianzhu Ma (senior author of Pocket2Mol; personal communication)**, sharpens why pocket-boundary sensitivity is expected to be *architecture-wide* rather than specific to diffusion. Autoregressive pocket-conditioned generators such as Pocket2Mol must **place a first atom** before any ligand context exists, and that initial placement is scored almost entirely from the surrounding pocket atoms (a frontier/initialization step conditioned on pocket geometry). When the crop boundary moves, the very atoms that define the first-atom context change, so the **initialization shifts and the error propagates through the entire autoregressive trajectory** — a "first-atom context problem." Diffusion and autoregressive models thus reach the same vulnerability by different routes: both condition generation on a pocket tensor whose membership is set by an arbitrary cropping policy, and neither is trained to be invariant to that policy. This convergence is why we expect the crop-radius effect to generalize across the SBDD model landscape, and it motivates **boundary-randomized or boundary-marginalized training** (sampling the crop radius during training) as a concrete mitigation.

These accounts remain **mechanistic hypotheses**. Confirming which channel dominates — first-atom/initialization context, edge-mask changes, or conditioning-distribution shift — will require **targeted ablations** (fixed-graph extraction, controlled crop sweeps with held-out boundaries, and first-atom-placement probes for autoregressive models). Pinpointing the dominant locus within each architecture remains an essential direction for future work.

**Limitations.** Brittleness is defined through a **threshold τ** on empirical standard deviations across the four perturbation conditions; τ trades sensitivity against permissiveness, and we therefore report a full τ sweep on **normalized** metrics rather than a single headline. The crop-radius result is a **paired, within-pocket** effect tested with the Wilcoxon signed-rank statistic and bootstrap confidence intervals on two independent panels, which we regard as the primary, robust finding; the earlier all-column "rate 1.0" flag is retained only as a cautionary example of how unnormalized metrics distort threshold-based brittleness. Pockets were evaluated with **n = 20** molecules per condition (panel default), which stabilizes proportion-based metrics but underpowers rare-event estimation; an n = 50 subset check left conclusions unchanged. **Pocket2Mol coverage from the first adapter pass (13/47) is not a trustworthy model-limited estimate** — most failures were harness/input-format; the corrected full-panel re-benchmark is incomplete (see Results caveat and `paper/STATUS.md`). The perturbation suite is not exhaustive — alternative noise kernels, side-chain packing, and physico-chemically motivated mutations will surface additional behaviour [4] — and a single crop magnitude (±1.5 Å) was tested; a graded crop sweep would chart the full dose–response. The benchmark currently evaluates **one** architecture end-to-end on both panels; finishing corrected Pocket2Mol coverage and extending the paired crop-radius protocol to Pocket2Mol, TargetDiff [9], and ResGen [10] is the natural next step.

## Methods

**Featurization stress protocol.** Each pocket was exposed to four perturbation tags treated as chemistry-preserving **featurization stressors**: (1) *atom_shuffle*—pseudo-random permutation of atom ordering within the extracted pocket; (2) *coordinate_jitter*—additive bounded noise to atomic coordinates; (3) *crop_radius_plus_1.5*—expansion of the pocket crop radius by 1.5 Å relative to baseline; (4) *crop_radius_minus_1.5*—contraction by 1.5 Å. Crop adjustments change which heavy atoms enter the pocket tensor and are therefore **not** strict mathematical invariances of a fixed atom set; we treat them as realistic pocket-*definition* perturbations alongside ordering and coordinate noise. The unperturbed pocket defined the *original* reference condition for each target.

**Brittleness flag.** For each pocket *p*, let **x**(*k*, *c*) denote metric *k* on condition *c*, with *c* ranging over the four perturbation tags (the original condition is excluded from the dispersion). Let σ̂*k* be the sample standard deviation of {*x*(*k*, *c*)} across those four conditions. The pocket is flagged **brittle** at threshold τ if σ̂*k* > τ for any monitored metric *k*. We report two monitored sets. The **normalized** set — validity, uniqueness, mean QED, and standard deviation of QED — places every metric on a comparable [0,1] scale and is the **primary** brittleness view (`paper/normalized_brittleness.csv`; CLI `--normalized-only`). The **all-column** set additionally includes raw molecule counts (*n*total, *n*valid, unique valid count) and unnormalized synthetic-accessibility scores (mean/std SA); because these quantities have natural dispersion far above any [0,1]-tuned τ, the all-column flag is reported only as a cautionary secondary view (Figs. 1–4), with the **dominant driver** at fixed τ defined as the metric with largest σ̂*k* among those exceeding τ (ties broken lexicographically; Fig. 1).

**Brittleness rate** is the fraction of pockets with at least one brittle flag. Thresholds τ ∈ {0.05, 0.10, 0.15, 0.20} are reported (Fig. 5; Table 1). We adopt τ = 0.10 as a conservative operational default: it represents a ten-point swing on unitless proportion summaries such as validity or QED, and aligns with the |Δ| > 0.10 responsiveness cutoff used for meaningful mutations.

**Crop-radius effect and paired test.** For the pocket-boundary analysis we pair, within each pocket, the `crop_radius_minus_1.5` condition against the `original` condition and compute Δ = (crop − original) for mean QED and mean SA. We report mean and median Δ, the fraction of pockets with ΔQED < −0.05 (`paper/crop_radius_effect.csv`), and a **paired two-sided Wilcoxon signed-rank test** (SciPy `wilcoxon`, zero-differences dropped) on each Δ for both panels. Effect size is the matched-pairs **rank-biserial correlation** r = (R⁺ − R⁻)/(R⁺ + R⁻); we also report the normal-approximation z-based r = |Z|/√n. **95% confidence intervals** on the median (and mean) Δ are obtained by bootstrap resampling of pockets (10,000 draws). Outputs are written to `paper/crop_radius_wilcoxon.csv` by `analysis/crop_radius_wilcoxon.py` (Table 2).

**Pocket Boundary Sensitivity Index (PBSI).** Each perturbation condition is assigned a crop-radius offset Δr (Å) parsed from its tag (`original` → 0; `crop_radius_plus_x` → +x; `crop_radius_minus_x` → −x). For each pocket we fit, by ordinary least squares, mean QED(Δr) = a + b·Δr and take the slope **b** (QED units per Å) as the per-pocket boundary sensitivity; positive b means drug-likeness rises with radius (so contraction lowers QED). The **panel PBSI** is the median |b|; we also report the median signed slope and the fraction of pockets with b > 0. To express sensitivity relative to representational noise, we compute on each pocket the QED standard deviation across the featurization-noise conditions (atom_shuffle, coordinate_jitter) as a noise floor, and the **signal-to-noise ratio** SNR = (|b|·1.5 Å)/noise_std; we report the median SNR and the fraction of pockets with SNR > 1 (boundary swing exceeds the noise floor). With the standard three radii (baseline ±1.5 Å) this yields a coarse linear estimate plus a symmetric-triple curvature term QED(+) + QED(−) − 2·QED(0); a fine sweep (`analysis/make_crop_sweep_config.py`) additionally supports a quadratic fit and per-pocket critical-radius estimation. Implemented in `analysis/pocket_boundary_sensitivity.py`; outputs `paper/pocket_boundary_sensitivity.csv` and `paper/pocket_boundary_sensitivity_summary.csv` (Table 3).

**Pocket panel construction.** Two independent panels were drawn from the RCSB Protein Data Bank according to the benchmark configurations shipped with the repository [4]: a 47-complex panel and a non-overlapping 99-pocket panel (one of 100 targeted pockets yielded no pocket atoms within the extraction radius and was dropped).

**DiffSBDD setup.** DiffSBDD sampling used the repository adapter, specified checkpoints, and configuration defaults for timesteps and decoding [1]. For each pocket and each condition, the model generated *n* = **20** molecules (panel default); validity, uniqueness, valid-molecule counts, mean and standard deviation of QED, mean and standard deviation of synthetic accessibility [5], and allied tabulated fields were computed with RDKit-backed scripts shared across models [6].

**Pocket2Mol setup.** Pocket2Mol inference used the project bridge to `sample_for_pdb.py`, pretrained weights, and a reduced-beam configuration suitable for local GPU memory, with PYTHONPATH set to the Pocket2Mol repository root [2]. The corrected adapter supplies a **full protein PDB** and a **ligand-centered** bbox (the first-pass pocket-only path produced the superseded 13/47 coverage figure). A full 47-pocket corrected coverage panel is not yet complete; *n* and tags match the panel comparator where runs exist (https://github.com/Marooncoloredchair/PocketBench).

**Vina docking (optional).** When enabled in the driver configuration, each accepted RDKit 3D conformer is scored with **AutoDock Vina** [7] in a **20 Å** cube centered on **`pocket.ligand_centroid`** (from the same ligand anchor used for pocket extraction). Receptors are exported as PDB from the **heavy atoms present in the pocket tensor**, then converted to **PDBQT** with **Meeko** [8] as the primary path (Open Babel or MGLTools `prepare_receptor4.py` as fallbacks); ligands use **Meeko** preparation where available. The reported quantity is the **best** (most negative) binding affinity (kcal/mol) per molecule. **Vina** and **Meeko** are **optional** dependencies (`pip install vina meeko` or `pip install -e ".[vina]"`); runs without them leave docking scores empty.

**Metrics.** *Validity* is the fraction of sampled molecules that are graph-valid and chemically parsed as connected products under project rules. *Uniqueness* is the fraction of unique canonical SMILES among valid products. *Synthetic accessibility* follows RDKit’s SA score as implemented in the pipeline [5]. The **primary** brittleness aggregate uses the normalized metric subset (validity, uniqueness, mean/std QED); the all-column aggregate over raw counts and unnormalized SA is reported only as a cautionary secondary view.

**Statistical validation (supplement).** `analysis/statistical_validation.py` exports `paper/statistical_validation.csv`.

We retain paired pocket-level Wilcoxon signed-rank tests comparing validity under each invariant stress versus the shared reference pocket.

To ask whether coordinate jitter induces any move in pooled validity irrespective of sign, define d_p as the absolute difference between jitter conditional validity and original-pocket validity on pocket p. A one-sample Student t-test contrasts H0: expected d equals zero with H1: expected d exceeds zero; we report sample mean absolute deviation, a symmetric two-sided 95% CI for E[d], and the one-sided p-value (matching `onesample_t_mean_abs_validity_delta_jitter` in `statistical_validation.csv`).

Operational validity volatility across exactly the quartet of invariant stresses (`atom_shuffle`, `coordinate_jitter`, `crop_radius_plus_1.5`, `crop_radius_minus_1.5`) is summarized per pocket with `numpy.std(..., ddof=0)`, matching brittle-flag internals. Across pockets Wilcoxon's signed-rank procedure tests median sigma > 0 and reports the pooled median with *p* (`wilcoxon_validity_sigma_across_four_stresses`).

Brittleness rates versus fixed tau recycle `flag_invariant_brittleness` (Fig. 5); bootstrap resampling of covered-pocket labels with replacement yields the brittle-fraction point estimate plus the 2.5-97.5 percentile envelope (`bootstrap_brittleness_rate` rows).

Pure label permutations of fixed four-vector validity tuples leave sigma unchanged unless values trade pockets, so permutation evidence adopts Monte Carlo quartet resampling: pool the observable 47x4 validity tableau, redraw four IID samples with replacement for each pseudopocket row thousands of times, recompute sigma, and tally how often observed pocket sigma clears the unstructured 95th percentile benchmark (`permutation_iid_pool_validity_sigma_vs_p95_null`). Small fractions imply observed cohesion seldom reaches the unstructured upper-tail benchmark; interpret alongside Wilcoxon and tau classifications.


**Comparative analysis and figures.** Normalized brittleness and the crop-radius effect were generated with `analysis/recompute_normalized_brittleness.py` and `analysis/crop_radius_wilcoxon.py`; the cautionary all-column dominant-driver view with `analysis/dominant_brittleness_metric.py` (https://github.com/Marooncoloredchair/PocketBench). Line plots, bar charts, and CSV summaries were derived from per-condition metrics and robustness summaries (Figs. 1–6 and Tables 1–2).

**Code and data.** The benchmark pipeline, configuration files, and analysis scripts are available at https://github.com/Marooncoloredchair/PocketBench. Frozen metrics CSVs for all reported runs are included in the repository under `data/results/`. Large generation archives will be deposited on Zenodo with a citable DOI at release; until then, contact the corresponding author for bulk SDF access if the repository copy is insufficient.
